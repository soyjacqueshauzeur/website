Chat marketing:
turn your channels
into salespeople

By Jacques Hauzeur · Chatbots · 5 min read

Your customers are already in the chat. In many markets, more people message a business on WhatsApp than fill out a contact form. Chat marketing is the discipline of turning those conversations into a predictable, sellable channel — and automation is what makes it scale without turning into spam.

Why the chat is where the sale happens.

For millions of buyers, messaging apps are the internet. They ask for a price on WhatsApp, check a look on Instagram, confirm a booking on Messenger. The difference from email is intent: a message is a request waiting for an answer. [3]

Done well, chat marketing does three things: it replies instantly, it qualifies the lead, and it hands the hot prospect to a human at the right moment. [1]

What you automate — and what you never should.

Automate.

  • Instant first reply and welcome messages, 24/7, so nobody waits. [1]
  • FAQ and price ranges for the questions you answer every day.
  • Qualification: budget, timeline, city, service.
  • Booking and payment links delivered inside the thread.
  • Re-engagement: a timely reminder, not a generic blast.

Never automate.

  • A complex negotiation or an angry customer.
  • Final pricing on a custom project.
  • Anything that pretends to be human when it is not — labels and a clear handoff build trust. [3]

ManyChat vs n8n: two philosophies.

ManyChat is the fast lane: a visual builder with ready-made templates for Instagram, Messenger, WhatsApp and Telegram. A keyword-to-DM flow can be live in an afternoon. [1]

n8n is the control room: an open, node-based automation platform that connects your CRM, spreadsheets, AI models and internal tools. More setup, more power, no per-contact ceiling. [2]

  • Choose ManyChat for speed, marketing features and a non-technical team.
  • Choose n8n for custom logic, data ownership and many-app integrations.
  • Many teams use both: ManyChat for the conversation, n8n for the data behind it.

Three flows that pay for themselves.

  1. Lead magnet: an ad keyword opens a WhatsApp thread; the bot delivers the guide and asks two qualifying questions. [3]
  2. Booking: a question about availability becomes a calendar link plus an automatic reminder.
  3. Reactivation: quiet contacts get a useful nudge instead of a broadcast.

Best practices.

  • Get explicit consent before messaging anyone. [3]
  • Keep the first message under thirty seconds of reading.
  • Always offer a path to a human.
  • Measure conversation-to-sale, not just open rates.
  • One channel done well beats four half-built ones.

Conclusion.

Chat marketing is not about flooding inboxes with bots. It is about removing friction: replying fast, qualifying honestly and letting a human close. Start with one channel and one flow, measure it, then add the next. Your customers already opened the chat — meet them there.
