The third frame is
usually the one.

By Mira Halden·Archive·6 min read

We shot 36 frames for the Atrium SS25 lookbook. Frame 12 made the cover. Frame 3 made the editorial spread. Frame 18 made the wholesale presentation. The pattern held: the third frame in every setup was the one we kept.

The sequence.

Every setup follows a rhythm. Frame 1 is the test — exposure check, composition check, the subject settling. Frame 2 is the adjustment — the photographer moves, the subject relaxes, the light shifts slightly. Frame 3 is the first frame where nobody is thinking about the technical. The subject has forgotten the camera. The photographer has stopped checking the monitor. The light is doing its thing.

Why frame 3?

Frame 1: "Chin down. Shoulders back. Eyes here." The subject is performing.

Frame 2: "Good. Now breathe. Stay there." The subject is transitioning.

Frame 3: Silence. The subject is. The photographer sees. The shutter fires.

This is not mysticism. It is physiology. The human stress response to being photographed peaks at ~90 seconds. By frame 3 (assuming ~30s per frame with direction), the cortisol has dipped. The micro-expressions return. The posture naturalises. The eyes soften.

We do not shoot more frames to get more options. We shoot more frames to get past the performance.— Joon Park, Partner · Director of Work

The contact sheet proof.

We still print contact sheets. Physical ones. 30×40 cm, pinned to the archive wall. The archive room has 11 years of them. Walk the room and you will see the same pattern: red grease-pencil circles on frame 3, frame 6, frame 9. The multiples of three. The rhythm holds across portraiture, still life, architecture — even product, where the "subject" is an object and the "performance" is the stylist's hand placing it.

What to do about it.

  1. Budget for the burn. If you need 12 selects, shoot 36 frames minimum. The first third are the tax you pay to get to the real work.
  2. Do not show the client frame 1. Ever. The client will pick frame 1 because it is first. Hide the contact sheet until you have culled to the selects.
  3. Tell the subject. "The first few frames are just us finding it. You don't need to perform." Say it before frame 1. It works.
  4. Trust the rhythm. If frame 3 feels right, shoot frame 4, 5, 6 anyway. Sometimes frame 6 is the one. But it is never frame 1.

The exception.

Architecture. The building does not perform. The light moves. Frame 1 at 14:00 is different from frame 1 at 15:00. There, the first frame can be the one — if you timed the light right. But even then, we bracket. Three exposures. Three compositions. The third composition is usually the one.

The first third of every shoot is the tax you pay to get to the real work.

— Tomas Brivón, Founder · Director of Production
